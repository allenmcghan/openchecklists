# ParaPlane PM-2 — Emergency procedures

Version 0 — draft. Not validated. These are starting points, not substitutes
for training. Powered parachute emergencies happen fast and at low altitude.
Read and brief these on the ground.

---

## Engine failure — one engine

A powered parachute remains flyable on one engine. Climb rate decreases
significantly. The aircraft will drift toward the dead engine side.

1. Maintain airspeed — do not pull brake on the live-engine side to
   compensate; use light opposite brake to arrest drift
2. Reduce throttle on the live engine as needed to maintain control
3. Identify the nearest suitable landing area immediately
4. If altitude permits and cause is obvious (kill switch, fuel starvation):
   attempt a single restart; otherwise descend to land
5. **Do not attempt restart below 300 ft AGL**
6. Land at the nearest suitable area

---

## Engine failure — both engines

A powered parachute is a parachute. Loss of both engines is survivable.

1. Release brakes — fly the canopy in full glide
2. Identify a landing area now (glide ratio approximately 3:1 — at 500 ft
   AGL you have roughly 1,500 ft of ground track available)
3. Set up approach into wind where possible
4. Standard brake flare — no power required
5. **Do not attempt restart below 300 ft AGL**

Note: a powered parachute glides less efficiently than a free-flight canopy
because the trike weight is forward of the attachment point. Expect a steeper
glide path than the 3:1 estimate at low altitude and light loading.

---

## High CHT or EGT

If CHT exceeds 435 °F or EGT exceeds 1,250 °F on either engine:

1. Reduce power on the affected engine
2. Check enrichment / carb heat if equipped
3. Do not climb — reduce thermal load
4. If CHT continues rising: shut down that engine, proceed as single-engine
5. Land at the nearest suitable area
6. Do not attempt to fly with a suspected piston or cylinder problem

**Rapid EGT rise on one side** (50 °F or more per minute) is a seizure
precursor. Shut down the affected engine immediately.

---

## Asymmetric canopy or partial collapse

1. Release both brakes immediately — full symmetric flight
2. Apply gentle, symmetric brake input — do not yank
3. If partial collapse persists: pump brakes rhythmically (small, rapid
   inputs) to restore inflation; do not hold deep brake
4. Maintain forward speed — a stalled or mushing canopy will not reinflate
5. **Altitude is your margin.** If low, fly the canopy as-is to the ground;
   do not sacrifice altitude trying to recover the collapse

---

## Low fuel / fuel exhaustion

If the fuel endurance alert triggers (below 1.5 gal remaining):

1. Note position and identify the nearest landing area
2. Reduce power to minimum cruise — extend endurance
3. Begin descending toward the landing area while both engines are running
4. Land while fuel remains — do not wait for engines to quit
5. If an engine quits: proceed as single-engine failure above

---

## Abort on takeoff run

1. Kill switch OFF — both engines
2. Release throttle
3. Apply brakes to stop the cart
4. Collapse the canopy immediately — pull rear risers or run to one side
5. Remain in the cart until fully stopped and props have stopped
6. Identify and correct the cause before next attempt

---

## Canopy line tangle (on the ground, before launch)

1. Kill switch OFF before touching any lines near the prop
2. Do not launch with any line tangle — even a small one
3. Untangle and re-inspect before next flight attempt
