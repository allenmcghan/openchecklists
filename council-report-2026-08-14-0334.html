LLM Council — Validation of openchecklists.net

Five independent advisors + anonymized peer review + chairman synthesis · 2026-08-14

Question: Is openchecklists.net — a free all-in-one VFR pre-flight resource (live weather, NOTAMs, frequencies, runways, winds aloft, PIREPs, sun times, maps for ~19,400 US airports; an end-to-end flight planner producing a printable/emailable briefing; a library of unreviewed checklists) — ready for real pilots to rely on as a research aid, and what are the highest-priority issues?

Chairman's Verdict

Where the council agrees

  • Framing/trust is the core problem, not features. "Everything you need before you fly" over sections that read "No fuel data" and "PIREPs unavailable," plus a green VFR badge sitting above a buried "nothing here is approved data," trains pilots to trust the badge and skip the caveat. Contrarian, First-Principles and Outsider all landed here independently.
  • The disclaimer belongs IN the badge. Show VFR (unverified) / snapshot · not a briefing at the point of the claim, not in the footer.
  • The empty fuel step actively hurts credibility. Better to hide it than to show "No data" on every airport.
  • Quarantining the checklists is the right call — but their mere presence still carries risk.

Where the council clashes

Is it "ready"? The Executor says yes-after-a-few-fixes and treats fuel/PIREPs/checklists as accurately-labeled non-blockers. The Contrarian says no — cached weather that can show stale VFR, and unreviewed emergency checklists, are safety defects that make "rely on it" the wrong verb. They're both right about different verbs: ready to publish as a research aid (Executor) vs. ready for a pilot to rely on (Contrarian). The honest answer is the middle: ship it as an explicit pre-check, not as something to rely on.

SEO priority. Contrarian calls the 19k→1-page collapse a structural failure to fix now; Executor says don't touch it until routing + CI are done; First-Principles doesn't rate it. Resolution: it's real but downstream of routing.

Grow vs. fix. Expansionist wants community/EFB/fuel-layer expansion; every other advisor (and all three peer reviewers) say safety/trust first. Growth is the right map, wrong month.

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • Regulatory duty (14 CFR 91.103). The PIC is legally required to obtain a "proper" weather briefing from an official source. The site should route users to 1800wxbrief/ForeFlight as the authoritative step, not position them as competitors.
  • Checklist legal exposure. "UNREVIEWED" is notice of known unreliability (can worsen a negligence posture, not shield it), and checklists reproduced from POH/AFM may carry copyright issues. Get the disclaimer/licensing reviewed.
  • NOTAM currency & sourcing. A stale or unsourced NOTAM (TFR, runway closure) is arguably more safety-critical than fuel or PIREPs — and no advisor asked where NOTAMs come from or how old they are.
Recommendation: Ship as an explicit pre-check aid — not "rely on." The build is genuinely functional (validated end-to-end today), so don't retreat. But close the trust gap before promoting it: (1) qualify every live-data badge as unverified with a visible timestamp, (2) hide the empty fuel step, (3) make "get your official briefing at 1800wxbrief" a first-class call-to-action on airport and briefing pages, and (4) guarantee weather is never served stale (timestamp + no-cache on live data). Then pursue the Expansionist's real upside: the checklist library and the ultralight/PPG niche.
The one thing to do first: change the homepage promise and the weather badges from a claim of completeness to a claim of currency — replace "Everything you need before you fly" with a pre-check framing, and render every METAR/VFR badge as VFR · observed HH:MMZ · unverified — get an official briefing. It's the cheapest change that removes the single biggest liability (false confidence) and is honest about what the tool is.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian

SEO collapse is a structural failure: 19k indexable pages → one client-rendered template means the site is invisible on Google to the people it serves. The disclaimer is inverted — "nothing here is approved data" increases liability because pilots anchor on the green VFR badge and skip the caveat. The stale service worker is a safety defect, not a bug — cached weather can send a pilot into IMC. The fuel calculator is theater if fuel-on-board is partly fictional. Unreviewed checklists are still THERE; "UNREVIEWED" gets ignored and a wrong C172 emergency item could kill someone. None of this is fatal to the project existing; all of it is fatal to calling it ready for pilots.

The First Principles Thinker

The real question is "what problem, for whom?" The problem it solves is friction for the casual/student VFR pilot who wants a single URL, no login, no paywall, to answer "can I fly today?" As a ForeFlight replacement: not ready. As a 90-second sanity check before calling 1800wxbrief: ready now. The right frame is pre-check vs. authoritative check. Disclaimers are correct in content but wrong in placement — put it in the badge: "VFR (unverified)". Priorities: (1) badge language, (2) NOTAM source/lag transparency, (3) the empty fuel section destroys planner credibility — omit the step rather than show "No data." The checklist quarantine is the right call.

The Expansionist

The checklist library is the undervalued moat — a community-curated, versioned, rated, structured library for every aircraft type is genuinely rare. The powered-parachute/ultralight angle is blue ocean: GA apps target certified pilots; the owner's background gives authentic credibility in an underserved, growing segment — build an ultralight/grass-strip airport filter and own it. Flight schools are the distribution unlock. EFB integration (export the briefing as ForeFlight .fpl / Garmin) gets it into cockpits alongside paid tools instead of competing. A community fuel-price layer would be something even ForeFlight charges for. This is a community platform in a tool's clothing.

The Outsider

"Everything you need before you fly" contradicts landing on "No fuel data" and "PIREPs unavailable" — gaps presented as completeness. "UNREVIEWED" on every checklist is alarming — if I can't trust it, why is it here? The jargon wall (METAR, TAF, VFR, 925hPa) is dense and unexplained. "Verify against the current Chart Supplement" — what is that, where do I get it? The headline plus "nothing here is approved data" is an unresolvable trust contradiction. A newcomer bounces, an expert has better tools, and the newly-licensed middle audience is the one most likely to be confused by incomplete data under an authoritative headline.

The Executor

The SW is network-first, so the stale cache is a one-off, not structural. Ruthless priority: (1) Fix path-segment URLs via a Cloudflare Pages dashboard toggle — switch "Not found behavior" from SPA to standard 404 and the existing _redirects rules work; also fixes plan deep-links; 30 min, no code. (2) Commit the dirty working tree and wire wrangler into CI. (3) SW cache-busting: tie the cache name to a content hash. (4) Investigate the plan "Refresh → not found" before dismissing. Accept fuel/PIREPs/unreviewed checklists as accurately-labeled non-blockers. Don't touch SEO until routing + CI are done. Monday's first move: toggle the Pages SPA setting and test /airport/kbna/.

Peer-review highlights (anonymized round)

Consensus: All three reviewers rated the Contrarian strongest (it names the issues with real-world harm potential — stale-weather cache, disclaimer inversion, SEO invisibility) and the Expansionist the biggest blind spot (a growth-strategy answer to a safety-validation question; no risk model).

What the whole council missed (each reviewer surfaced one): (a) FAR 91.103 places a legal duty on the PIC to obtain an official briefing — the site should direct users there, not compete; (b) checklist copyright/negligence exposure — "UNREVIEWED" is notice of unreliability and POH/AFM reproductions may be unlicensed; (c) NOTAM currency and sourcing — potentially the most safety-critical data on the site and no one asked where it comes from or how old it is.
