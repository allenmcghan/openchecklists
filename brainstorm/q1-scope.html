Flight Planner Design — Question 1 of ~6

What flight types should the planner support at launch?

This is the most important scope decision. VFR and IFR planning have very different workflows, data needs, and complexity. Pick what fits your primary audience.

A

VFR Only — start focused

Visual flight rules planning: departure airport, route waypoints, destination, alternate. Pulls weather (METAR/TAF), NOTAMs, airspace, fuel from the existing airport pages. Student pilots and fair-weather fliers covered immediately.

✓ Recommended — ships fast, serves the majority
B

VFR + Basic IFR — instrument-aware

Everything in A, plus: IFR alternate requirements, instrument approach plate links, fuel reserve rules (45 min IFR vs 30 min VFR), and filed route format (DCT/airways). Does not file plans directly with FAA yet.

Broader audience; 30–40% more complexity
C

Full IFR + FAA Filing — complete platform

Everything in B, plus direct FAA DUATS/Leidos FlightService integration to file the plan. Includes SID/STAR routing, preferred routes, EFB-quality briefing. Very ambitious for v1; a near-complete flight software product.

⚠ High risk — 4–6× scope of option A
